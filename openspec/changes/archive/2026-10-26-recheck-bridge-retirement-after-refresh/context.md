The two observed pre-cutover failures were soft prompt-cache lookups: `reuse pending=0`, a retiring 502, old bridge close, and cancellation of an older long-lived response at the same timestamp. A subsequent external retry succeeded. These logs are compatible with the reproduced race; they do not record the precise assignment of the retirement flag, so causality remains bounded to that compatible code path.

The public-route regression uses the real cancellation detach method during the real registry lookup's lease-refresh await. Lookup must reconsider retirement before returning the session. This is replacement before submission, not a retry of a response.create frame. The final submit guard remains necessary if retirement starts later, after the lookup returns. Explicit client anchors must remain unchanged and their account constraints must continue through ordinary replacement selection. A reserved unanchored handoff already owns its predecessor and follows the existing detached-generation dispatch rules.

The replacement path must use its drain-aware detach: an accepted visible sibling retains the old physical socket and pending ownership until its terminal settlement. Falling through the durable-owner mismatch's unconditional close was caught during review and reproduced by a second public-route RED test, then corrected before release.

Verification covers unauthenticated and limited-key requests, with and without a visible sibling, and explicit anchors under soft or hard owner affinity. The limited key has exactly one reservation: successful replacement finalizes it; owner-bound rejection releases it. The unanchored positive case records one physical create attempt, zero replay count and no continuity operation row; anchored negative cases have zero physical create attempts, do not enter account selection and retain the client anchor. The reserved-handoff regression remains on the predecessor even when retirement is observed during refresh.

The final submit fence still rejects retirement that begins after lookup returns. This change does not claim every possible `bridge is retiring` response is eliminated. The separate previous-response-alias refresh path and post-lookup races remain outside this narrow fix.

An isolated actual-helper microbenchmark stubs durable I/O and event logging, runs 11 interleaved samples of 100,000 awaits, and compares the base helper to this implementation: median CPU time 1,273.96 ns versus 1,382.48 ns per call (+108.52 ns, +8.52%). This is not end-to-end gateway latency. The common path adds a retirement-flag check and boolean return, with no new retained state, buffers or cache. Database, native protocol and runtime configuration do not change; no native helper rebuild is required.
